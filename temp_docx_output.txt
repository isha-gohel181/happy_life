Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the transfer of heat? 
The heat flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature. 
In solids, heat is typically transferred through conduction. 
In liquids and gases, the heat is transferred by convection. 
Transfer of heat by radiation is not possible without the presence of a medium.
The incorrect statement is:
d) Transfer of heat by radiation is not possible without the presence of a medium.
Explanation:
Heat can be transferred in three ways:
Conduction
Mainly occurs in solids.
Heat passes from particle to particle without actual movement of the substance.
Hence, statement (b) is correct.
Convection
Occurs in liquids and gases.
Heat is transferred by the actual movement of particles.
Hence, statement (c) is correct.
Radiation
Heat is transferred in the form of electromagnetic waves.
It does not require any medium.
Example: Heat from the Sun reaches Earth through the vacuum of space.
Therefore, statement (d) is incorrect.
Also, heat naturally flows from a hotter body to a colder body, so statement (a) is correct.
Correct Answer:
d) Transfer of heat by radiation is not possible without the presence of a medium.

Hooke’s Law 
Newton’s Third Law of Motion 
Coulomb’s Law
The correct answer is:

Explanation:
Newton’s Third Law of Motion states that:
For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
In the case of a train:
The wheels push the railway tracks backward.
The tracks exert an equal and opposite forward force on the wheels.
This reaction force moves the train forward.

b) Hooke’s Law → Related to elasticity of springs.
d) Coulomb’s Law → Related to electrostatic force between charges.
Correct Answer:
c) Newton’s Third Law of Motion

Consider the following statements: 
Indian Council Act of 1919 allowed the provincial legislative councils to have non-official majority for the first time. 
The Government of India Act of 1935 introduced bicameralism and direct elections in the country for the first time. 
The Indian trade was thrown open to all British merchants for the first time through the Charter Act of 1813. 
How many of the statements given above are correct? 
Only one
Only two
All three
None
The correct answer is a) Only one.
Here is the step-by-step evaluation of each statement:
Statement 1 is Incorrect
The Fact: It was the Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms) that allowed provincial legislative councils to have a non-official majority for the first time.
The Government of India Act of 1919 further expanded on provincial governance by introducing Dyarchy, but the non-official majority milestone belongs to 1909.
Statement 2 is Incorrect
The Fact: Bicameralism and direct elections at the central level were introduced for the first time by the Government of India Act of 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms), not the 1935 Act.
The 1919 Act replaced the Indian Legislative Council with a bicameral legislature consisting of a Council of State (Upper House) and a Legislative Assembly (Lower House). The 1935 Act merely extended bicameralism to six provinces.
Statement 3 is Correct
The Fact: The Charter Act of 1813 ended the commercial monopoly of the East India Company in India, effectively throwing Indian trade open to all British merchants.
Note: The Company did retain its monopoly over trade in tea and trade with China (which were later completely abolished by the Charter Act of 1833), but the statement accurately reflects the general opening up of Indian trade.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct? 
1 and 2 only
2 only 
1 only 
1, 2 and 3
The correct answer is:
c) 1 only
Explanation:
Statement 1:
“A mandamus is issued to an office holder who is not doing his legal duty.”
This statement is correct.
The writ of Mandamus means “we command.”
It is issued by a court to a public authority or office holder directing them to perform their legal duty.
Statement 2:
“In Quo Warranto, the court orders a lower court to transfer a matter to a higher court.”
This statement is incorrect.
This description relates more closely to Certiorari, not Quo Warranto.
Quo Warranto means “by what authority.”
It is issued to question the legality of a person holding a public office.
Statement 3:
“Certiorari is issued when a person holds an office he is not entitled to.”
This statement is incorrect.
This function belongs to Quo Warranto.
Certiorari is issued by a higher court to quash the order or decision of a lower court or tribunal when it acts beyond its jurisdiction.
